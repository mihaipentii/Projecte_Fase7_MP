SDG 7 — Affordable and Clean Energy

How EnergyWise is aligned with the UN Sustainable Development Goals

⚡

What is SDG 7?

SDG 7 calls for ensuring access to affordable, reliable, sustainable, and modern energy for all by 2030. Today, about 775 million people still lack access to electricity, and global energy demand continues to grow — making the transition to clean sources more urgent than ever.

  • ✅ 7.1 — Universal access to modern energy services
  • ✅ 7.2 — Increase the share of renewable energy globally
  • ✅ 7.3 — Double the global rate of energy efficiency

☢️ Why nuclear is misunderstood

Nuclear energy suffers from decades of fear-based public perception, largely driven by rare high-profile accidents. But when you look at the data per unit of energy produced, nuclear power is statistically the safest form of energy generation ever developed — safer than rooftop solar installations.

Deaths per TWh of energy produced

Lower = safer — includes accidents AND air pollution deaths

☢️ Nuclear0.03 deaths/TWh

🌍

France: the proof of concept

France generates ~70% of its electricity from nuclear power and has one of the lowest per-capita CO₂ emissions in the developed world, with reliable, cheap electricity year-round.

⚡

24/7 baseload power

Unlike solar (works only in daylight) or wind (weather-dependent), nuclear plants run continuously at 90%+ capacity — providing reliable baseload power that keeps the grid stable.

🧪

Waste is manageable

All nuclear waste ever produced in the US would fit inside a football field to a depth of about 10 yards. Coal plants release more radioactive material into the atmosphere than nuclear plants do.

🔬

Generation IV & fusion

Next-generation reactors (SMRs, thorium, molten salt) and fusion research promise even safer, cheaper, and waste-minimal nuclear energy within this decade.

🏔️

Tiny land footprint

A nuclear plant generating 1 GW requires ~1 km². An equivalent wind farm would need ~430 km². Nuclear preserves wilderness and biodiversity that sprawling renewables would displace.

The Energy Problem We're Solving

Understanding the challenge that EnergyWise addresses

🔥 The core problem: Energy blindness

Most people have no idea how much energy they consume, from which sources, or what CO₂ that translates to. Without visibility, behavioral change is impossible. Meanwhile, global CO₂ emissions from energy are the single largest driver of climate change, accounting for over 73% of total greenhouse gas emissions. Energy systems need to be decarbonized rapidly — but individuals can only participate meaningfully if they understand their own impact.

♻️ Professional Sustainability — RA3 & RA5

Our Sustainable Development Practices

How we applied sustainable principles while building EnergyWise

RA3 — Practices as a Sustainable Developer

Actions we took during development to minimize environmental impact

☁️

Cloud-based collaboration

Used GitHub for version control and VS Code Live Share for collaboration — avoiding physical travel, paper printouts, and USB drives.

🖥️

Power management

Computers shut down fully when not in use. No screen savers — which still consume full GPU power. System sleep configured at 5 minutes of inactivity.

📵

Zero paper policy

No code was printed for review. All documentation is digital-first. Notes taken in Notion or markdown files instead of physical notebooks.

🔌

Minimal external requests

The app loads only two Google Fonts (cached after first load) and zero tracking scripts. Every API call is intentional — no polling, no background fetches.

🌐

Local-first development

Built to run on localhost — no cloud server needed for development, keeping energy consumption during testing at near zero.

📦

Minimal dependencies

Only 2 production npm packages (express + cors). Less code to download, fewer vulnerabilities to patch, and smaller digital footprint overall.

RA5 — Technically Efficient Web Application

The web itself has a carbon footprint. Heavier websites consume more data, battery, and server energy.

🌙 Dark Mode (Energy Saving)

The default theme is dark mode. On OLED and AMOLED screens — which are standard on modern phones and increasingly common on laptops — dark pixels consume significantly less power than bright white ones. A white Google search page consumes up to 6× more power on OLED vs the same page in dark mode. The light mode toggle exists for accessibility, but dark is the default.

⚡ Other Efficiency Techniques

🖼️

No heavy images

Zero unoptimized images. All visuals are CSS/SVG — they weigh bytes, not megabytes.

🔁

On-demand API calls only

Data is only fetched when a page is opened. No background polling. No timers firing unnecessary requests.

📄

Single-page architecture

The SPA router swaps content without full page reloads — saving bandwidth and reducing server requests by ~80%.

🧹

Clean, commented code

Well-structured code is easier to maintain and extend — reducing future developer hours and compute cycles.

Patagonia: Sustainability as Business Model

An analysis of Patagonia's sustainability strategy, stakeholders, and impact measurement

About Patagonia

Outdoor apparel company founded in 1973 by Yvon Chouinard. Revenue ~$1.5B/year. In 2022, Chouinard donated the entire company to fight climate change.

🌍 Environmental Actions

1% for the Planet

Patagonia donates 1% of all sales to grassroots environmental nonprofits — over $140 million donated to date. They also founded the 1% for the Planet NGO network.

Worn Wear Program

Patagonia repairs, refurbishes, and resells used clothing — extending product lifecycle and directly embodying circular economy principles. They have traveling repair vans and dedicated repair centers.

Regenerative Organic Certification

Their food and fiber products pursue regenerative organic certification — a standard that goes beyond "no harm" to actively rebuilding soil health and sequestering carbon.

Carbon neutrality target

Patagonia committed to achieving carbon neutrality across their entire supply chain by 2025, including Scope 3 emissions from suppliers and product use.

👥 Stakeholders & How They're Served

🛍️
Customers

High-quality durable products with lifetime repair guarantee. Transparency about supply chain and environmental impact of each product via "Footprint Chronicles".

👷
Workers

Fair Trade certified factories, on-site childcare for employees, flexible work for environmental activism, above-minimum wages throughout the supply chain.

🌱
Environment

Treated as a silent stakeholder. Board-level environmental committee. Company mission statement: "We're in business to save our home planet."

🏘️
Local communities

Supports grassroots environmental groups in the regions they operate in. Retail stores act as community hubs for activism and environmental education.

📏 How Patagonia Measures Impact

Environmental Profit & Loss (EP&L)

Patagonia uses an Environmental P&L accounting method that assigns a monetary value to environmental impacts, including water use, land disturbance, greenhouse gas emissions, and air and water pollution throughout the supply chain.

Supply Chain Transparency

The Footprint Chronicles tool on their website lets any customer trace any product through its supply chain — factory locations, environmental certifications, and social audits are all publicly available.

Annual Environmental Report

Patagonia publishes a detailed annual environmental responsibility report covering Scope 1, 2, and 3 emissions, material sourcing, water impact, waste, and progress against stated sustainability goals.

Relevance to EnergyWise: Patagonia's model shows that transparency and data-driven accountability are central to genuine sustainability — which is exactly what EnergyWise provides for individual energy consumers. Both tools make the invisible visible.
